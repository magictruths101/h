FOOTNOTE 8 prettiness
Page 112 - Ophelia’s final act is to make the situation around her pretty. She is unable to fix anything, and she promptly dies by suicide after this, capturing the constraints put on women of this time. She was never given an opportunity to seek revenge or even acceptance. When she experienced loss, all she could do was make it pretty.

FOOTNOTE 8 joy
Page 112 - Ophelia continues on this trend with her last song singing of joy. Instead of working through her grief, she tries to mask it. By trying to cover up her problems, she is unable to grapple with them, and they end up defeating her, driving her to suicide.

FOOTNOTE 9 due content
Page 113 - Claudius speaks of satisfying the urges that plague Laertes. This strengthens the overarching theme of the novel that fueling negative emotions like grief instead of moving past them leads to destruction. In trying to satisfy their desire for revenge, Hamlet and Laertes lose their lives. Laertes and Ophelia act as foils for each other, with both acting on the drastic ends of the spectrum of grief, and neither of them trying to process it naturally.
